# Phrase bank

Starter set. Replace or extend with your own terms; drafts written with `/lit write` draw on this file.

The phrase follows the relation, never the other way round: decide from the matrix whether two sources agree or conflict, then pick the wording.

X, Y and Z stand for your own terms.

## Synthesis

| Relation | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Similarity | similarly; likewise; in the same vein; in line with; consistent with; echoes; corroborates; this view is supported by X, who found that; X's work on A is complemented by Y's study of B |
| Contrast | in contrast (to X); unlike X; whereas; whilst X focused on A, Y was more concerned with B; conversely; challenges; diverges from; however, Y's study found no link between; a broader perspective has been adopted by |
| Extension | builds on; extends; refines; generalises to; in a follow-up study, X found that |
| Qualification | holds only when; under the condition that; with the caveat that |
| Gap | remains unexplored; has received little attention; has not been examined in; much less is known about; remains unclear |
| Cause | consequently; as a result; this accounts for |
| Addition (same direction) | moreover; furthermore; also |

Transition check: "on the other hand" and "in contrast" need a real contrast with the previous sentence; to add a supporting point use "moreover" or "furthermore". "On the contrary" denies the claim or expectation just stated; it is not a synonym for "in contrast".

## Reporting

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Method | was conducted; were recruited; was measured using; was analysed by |
| Result | showed; indicated; revealed; no significant difference was found |
| Strength of claim | demonstrates (strong); suggests (moderate); may indicate (weak) |
| Limitation | is limited by; should be interpreted with caution; cannot be generalised to |
| Author's position | argued; claimed; maintained; asserted; concluded; pointed out; noted; observed; proposed; postulated; questioned; challenged |
| What a text does | identifies; outlines; highlights; draws on; draws attention to; discusses; defines; traces; lists; considers whether |
| Collective findings | several studies have found / reported / shown that / indicated that / linked X with Y / confirmed / revealed a correlation between |

Tense: present perfect for the literature in general ("several studies have examined"); simple past for one dated study ("Smith (2012) found"); present tense for ideas that still hold ("Smith argues").

## Evaluating previous work

Across studies:

- Most studies in the field of X have only focused on ...
- The research to date has tended to focus on X rather than Y.
- Such approaches, however, have failed to address ...
- Much of the research up to now has been descriptive in nature.
- The generalisability of much published research on this issue is problematic.
- Previously published studies on the effect of X are not consistent.
- Previous studies cannot be considered conclusive because ...
- All the studies reviewed so far, however, suffer from the fact that ...
- The existing accounts fail to resolve the contradiction between X and Y.

One study:

- The main weakness of the study is the failure to address how ...
- No attempt was made to quantify the association between X and Y.
- The study fails to consider ...; does not take into account ...
- The paper would appear to be over-ambitious in its claims.
- The paper does not / makes no attempt to: specify; quantify; compare; account for; distinguish between; address the question of; assess the effectiveness of; offer an adequate explanation for; give sufficient consideration to
- X's argument relies too heavily on ...; X's analysis does not take account of ...

Method or theory:

- One major drawback of this approach is that ...
- Selection bias is another potential concern because ...
- This method of analysis has a number of limitations.
- The main weakness with this theory is that ...; this theory does not fully explain why ...
- One question that needs to be asked, however, is whether ...

Constructive:

- The study would have been more useful / convincing / relevant if it had included ...
- A more comprehensive study would include ...
- A much more systematic approach would identify how X interacts with ...

Positive evaluation (use only when the evidence earns it): seminal; influential; pioneering; comprehensive; thorough; detailed; timely; innovative; wide-ranging

## Stating the gap

Little or no research:

- No previous study has investigated X.
- There has been little quantitative analysis of ...; no detailed investigation of ...
- So far, very little attention has been paid to the role of X.
- Few studies have investigated X in any systematic way.
- The impact of X on Y is understudied, particularly for ...
- Despite the importance of X, there remains a paucity of evidence on ...
- To date, the problem has received scant attention in the research literature.
- Although extensive research has been carried out on X, no single study exists which ...
- While some research has been carried out on X, there have been few empirical investigations into ...
- To the author's knowledge, no previous research has investigated ...

Something not yet known:

- It is still not known whether ...; What is not yet clear is the impact of X on ...
- The nature of X remains unclear; X is not fully understood / poorly understood.
- A systematic understanding of how X contributes to Y is still lacking.
- Much uncertainty still exists about the relationship between ...
- This indicates a need to understand ...

Filling it:

- This study addresses the need for X, so far lacking in the literature.
- To fill this gap, this study identifies ...
- Previous research can only be considered a first step towards a more profound understanding of ...

A gap phrase is a claim about absence. Under the `/lit` rules it needs its own targeted search before it goes into a draft.

## Your own study: contribution, implications, limits

Contribution:

- This study provides the first comprehensive assessment of ...
- This work contributes to existing knowledge of X by providing ...
- The present study adds to the growing body of research that indicates ...
- This study has gone some way towards enhancing our understanding of ...
- The present study lays the groundwork for future research into ...
- The findings reported here shed new light on ...
- The methods used for this X may be applied to other Xs elsewhere.

Implications:

- The findings of this study suggest that ...; Taken together, these results suggest that ...
- An implication of this is the possibility that ...
- These findings have significant implications for the understanding of how ...
- This finding, while preliminary, suggests that ...
- The findings of this investigation complement those of earlier studies.
- Although this study focuses on X, the findings may well have a bearing on ...

Limitations:

- A limitation of this study is that ...; The major limitation of this study is the ...
- The scope of this study was limited in terms of ...
- The small sample size did not allow ...
- An issue that was not addressed in this study was whether ...
- The generalisability of these results is subject to certain limitations. For instance, ...
- The responses relating to X were subjective and were therefore susceptible to recall bias.

Caution:

- These results therefore need to be interpreted with caution.
- A note of caution is due here since ...
- These findings cannot be extrapolated to all ...
- With a small sample size, caution must be applied, as the findings might not be ...
- It could be argued that the positive results were due to ...

Limitation and contribution in one sentence:

- Notwithstanding these limitations, the study suggests that ...
- Despite its exploratory nature, this study offers some insight into ...
- Although the current study is based on a small sample of participants, the findings suggest ...

## Hedging

## Formal word choice

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| find out | discover; determine; establish |
| look into | investigate; examine |
| talk about | discuss |
| come up with | develop; propose |
| go up / go down | increase / decrease |
| set up | establish |
| put together | assemble |
| bring about | effect; produce |
| get | collect; gain; obtain |
| make | produce; construct |
| let | allow; permit |
| say; show (overused) | outline; demonstrate; maintain; conclude; note; argue |
| a couple of; lots of; a lot of | several; a great number of; a variety of |
| big | substantial; significant |
| good / bad | positive; considerable / negative |
| thing; stuff | the specific noun |
| people | participants; experts; the specific group |
| sort of | rather; somewhat |
| like | such as; for example |
| very; really; so | omit, or use a stronger word |
| etc.; and so on | and other + noun |
| not many; not any | few; no |
| don't; isn't; can't | do not; is not; cannot |

Sentence habits:

- Follow "this" or "these" with a summary noun: "This understanding can help", not "This can help".
- Cut an opening "There is / There are" where the sentence survives without it.
- Do not open a sentence with and, but or so.
- Do not address the reader as "you", and do not ask the reader questions (a research question is the exception).
- Use the passive when who did it does not matter, but do not make it the default.
